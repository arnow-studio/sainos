Clementine

The Corsican clementine PGI is recognisable by the leaves left on the fruit.

  • Season: November, December, January, February
  • Peak season: December, January
  • Botanical family: Rutaceae

History

The clementine is named after Brother Clément (Vital Rodier), who discovered it around 1902 in the garden of an orphanage near Oran, Algeria. Probably a cross between a mandarin and an orange tree, it was planted in Corsica from the 1920s.

Origin and growing regions

French clementines are grown in Corsica, mainly on the eastern plain:

  • Corsica (eastern plain)

Corsican clementines have a PGI: they are picked ripe and sold with their leaves, a sign of freshness.

Benefits

The clementine is winter’s treat fruit:

  • Vitamin C: Two clementines cover a good share of daily needs.
  • Moderate energy: About 47 kcal per 100 g.
  • Fibre: In the membranes of the segments.
  • Handy: Easy to peel, perfect for snacks.

Varieties

  • Corsican clementine: PGI, sold with its leaves.
  • Oronules: Early, from October.
  • Clemenules: Big and juicy, the most grown in Spain.
  • Hernandina: Late, until February.

Zero waste

  • Storing: Somewhere cool, in a bowl: 1 to 2 weeks. A little longer in the fridge.
  • Freezing: In segments or as juice: up to 6 months.
  • Nothing goes to waste: Dried peel flavours herbal tea or a cake. Candied, it makes treats.
