Mint

Invasive in the garden, fresh in cooking and in infusions.

  • Season: May, June, July, August, September
  • Peak season: June, July
  • Botanical family: Lamiaceae

History

Mint, native to the Mediterranean and Asia, has been used since ancient times. In Greek mythology, the nymph Minthe was turned into a mint plant. The Romans used it to scent their baths and sauces.

Origin and growing regions

French mint is grown in regions known for aromatic plants:

  • Île-de-France (Milly-la-Forêt)
  • Pays de la Loire (Anjou)
  • Provence

Milly-la-Forêt has long been France’s peppermint capital.

Benefits

Mint brings instant freshness:

  • Menthol: Mainly in peppermint, it creates a cooling sensation.
  • Digestion: Traditionally drunk as an infusion after meals.
  • Versatile: As good in savoury dishes as in desserts.
  • Flavour without salt: It lifts salads and tabbouleh.

Varieties

  • Spearmint: The most common, for tabbouleh.
  • Peppermint: Very fresh, for infusions.
  • Moroccan mint: The one for mint tea.
  • Chocolate mint: Smells of mint chocolate.
  • Apple mint: Fuzzy leaves, mild taste.

Zero waste

  • Storing: As a bunch in a glass of water, or in the fridge in a damp tea towel: 1 week.
  • Freezing: Chopped in ice cubes with water: up to 6 months, perfect for drinks.
  • Nothing goes to waste: The stems make an infusion. Wilted mint can be dried for tea.
