Strawberry

Its real fruits are the little grains on its surface: the achenes.

  • Season: April, May, June, July
  • Peak season: May, June
  • Botanical family: Rosaceae

History

Wild strawberries have been eaten in Europe since ancient times. The big garden strawberry was born in Brittany in the 18th century, from a cross between a Chilean strawberry brought back in 1714 by the officer Amédée-François Frézier and a Virginia strawberry. Plougastel then became its capital.

Origin and growing regions

France grows strawberries in several major areas:

  • Nouvelle-Aquitaine (Lot-et-Garonne, Périgord)
  • Brittany (Plougastel)
  • Occitanie (Nîmes)
  • Provence

Périgord and Nîmes strawberries have a PGI.

Benefits

The strawberry is as light as it is delicious:

  • Very rich in vitamin C: A 150 g serving covers daily needs.
  • Low in calories: About 33 kcal per 100 g.
  • Antioxidants: Its red pigments are anthocyanins.
  • Hydrating: About 90 % water.

Varieties

  • Gariguette: Elongated and tangy, the first of the season.
  • Ciflorette: Very fragrant and sweet.
  • Mara des bois: Tastes like wild strawberries, until autumn.
  • Charlotte: Round and sweet, picked all summer.
  • Wild strawberry: Tiny and very fragrant.

Zero waste

  • Storing: In the fridge, unwashed and with stalks on, on kitchen paper: 2 to 3 days. Take them out 30 min before eating.
  • Freezing: Hulled, spread out on a tray then bagged: up to 12 months.
  • Nothing goes to waste: Damaged strawberries make a sauce, jam or smoothie. The stalks flavour infused water.
