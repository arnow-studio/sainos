Parsley

Flat or curly, a botanical cousin of the carrot.

  • Season: March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November
  • Peak season: May, June
  • Botanical family: Apiaceae

History

Native to the eastern Mediterranean, parsley was used by the Greeks to weave crowns before the Romans turned it into a cooking herb. Charlemagne listed it among the plants to be grown in the gardens of his estates.

Origin and growing regions

Parsley is grown almost everywhere in France:

  • Provence-Alpes-Côte d’Azur
  • Brittany
  • Île-de-France

Benefits

Parsley is much more than a garnish:

  • Vitamin C: It contains a lot, as long as it is eaten raw.
  • Vitamin K: Present in large amounts.
  • Chlorophyll: It freshens the breath.
  • Flavour without salt: Best chopped and added at the end.

Varieties

  • Flat-leaf parsley: More fragrant, for cooking.
  • Curly parsley: Milder, often used as a garnish.
  • Root parsley: Its root is eaten, like a parsnip.

Zero waste

  • Storing: As a bunch in a glass of water in the fridge: 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Freezing: Chopped, in a bag: up to 6 months. Crumble it straight from frozen.
  • Nothing goes to waste: The stems flavour stocks and soups. Wilted parsley can be blended into a green sauce.
