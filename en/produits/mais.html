Sweetcorn

Sweet kernels to grill or boil right after picking, before the sugar turns to starch.

  • Season: August, September, October
  • Peak season: August, September
  • Botanical family: Poaceae

History

Maize was domesticated in Mexico about 9,000 years ago from a wild grass called teosinte. A staple food of the Maya and the Aztecs, it reached Europe with Christopher Columbus. Sweetcorn, which is sweeter, comes from a natural mutation and became popular mainly in the United States.

Origin and growing regions

France is Europe’s leading sweetcorn producer, grown mostly in the south-west:

  • Nouvelle-Aquitaine (Landes)
  • Occitanie

Benefits

Sweetcorn is tasty and filling:

  • Energy: Rich in carbohydrates, it keeps you full for a long time.
  • Fibre: Found in the skin of the kernels.
  • Lutein and zeaxanthin: Yellow pigments that support eye health.
  • Gluten-free: Suitable for people with gluten intolerance.

Varieties

  • Sweetcorn: Sweet kernels, for grilling or boiling.
  • Popcorn maize: Hard kernels that pop in the heat.
  • Field maize: For flour and animal feed.
  • Blue corn: Blue-purple kernels, for colourful flours.

Zero waste

  • Storing: In the fridge, in its husk: 2 to 3 days. It loses its sugar very quickly.
  • Freezing: As cobs blanched for 4 min, or as kernels: up to 12 months.
  • Nothing goes to waste: The cobs (the core of the ear) flavour a stock. Extra kernels go into salads, fritters or soup.
