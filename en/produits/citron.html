Lemon

A sour citrus whose zest adds as much flavour as its juice.

  • Season: November, December, January, February, March, April, May
  • Peak season: January, February, March
  • Botanical family: Rutaceae

History

Native to Asia (north-east India, Burma, China), the lemon reached the Mediterranean with the Arabs in the Middle Ages. In the 18th century, British sailors took lemons on board to fight scurvy, a disease caused by a lack of vitamin C.

Origin and growing regions

Lemons sold in France come mostly from Spain and Italy, with a small, renowned French production:

  • Spain (Murcia)
  • Italy (Sicily)
  • France (Menton)

Menton lemons have a PGI.

Benefits

The lemon adds acidity and vitamins:

  • Vitamin C: Especially in fresh juice.
  • Citric acid: It lifts dishes and stops cut fruit from browning.
  • Very low in calories: About 29 kcal per 100 g.
  • Fragrant zest: Rich in essential oils; choose unwaxed lemons.

Varieties

  • Eureka: Yellow and tart, the most common.
  • Primofiore: Early, thin skin.
  • Verdelli: Picked in summer, greenish skin.
  • Menton lemon: PGI, not very sour, very fragrant.

Zero waste

  • Storing: At room temperature: 1 week. In the fridge, in a box: 3 to 4 weeks.
  • Freezing: As juice in an ice-cube tray, as zest or in slices: up to 6 months.
  • Nothing goes to waste: A squeezed lemon half cleans and deodorises the chopping board. Zest can be frozen for later.
