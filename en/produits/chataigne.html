Chestnut

A nut from the forests of Ardèche and the Cévennes, to roast or turn into spread.

  • Season: October, November, December
  • Peak season: October, November
  • Botanical family: Fagaceae

History

The chestnut tree has grown in southern Europe since ancient times. In the Cévennes, Ardèche and Corsica it was called “the bread tree”: for centuries, its dried and ground fruit fed mountain communities.

Origin and growing regions

French chestnuts grow in mid-mountain regions:

  • Ardèche
  • Cévennes
  • Corsica
  • Limousin

Ardèche chestnuts have a PDO, as does Corsican chestnut flour.

Benefits

The chestnut is a very nourishing winter fruit:

  • Complex carbohydrates: Lasting energy, about 180 kcal per 100 g cooked.
  • Fibre: Helps you feel full.
  • Gluten-free: Its flour suits people with gluten intolerance.
  • Minerals: Potassium, magnesium and B vitamins.

Varieties

  • Marron: Big nut with a single seed, for marrons glacés.
  • Bouche de Bétizac: Big and easy to peel.
  • Comballe: From Ardèche, fragrant, for flour.
  • Ardèche chestnut: PDO, mild and sweet.

Zero waste

  • Storing: In the fridge, in a bag with holes: 1 week. They dry out quickly in the air.
  • Freezing: Raw and slit, or peeled after cooking: up to 12 months.
  • Nothing goes to waste: Extra chestnuts can be turned into chestnut spread, purée or flour.
